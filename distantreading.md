**CoPilot Prompts and Replies Summarization**

Me: "Please analyze Jane Austen's novels. Include most popular ones, their plots, character descriptions, summaries, analyzations, and themes. make this as thorough as possible while staying as accurate as possible."

CoPilot: Focused on six of Jane Austen's works: "Sense and Sensibility", "Pride and Prejudice", "Mansfield Park", "Emma", "Northanger Abbey", and "Persuasion". Their publishing years were listed from 1811-1818, as well as main characters and tone/focus. This was represented in a chart. 

CoPilot: For each of the six books, there are three sections: a plot summary, list of 5-7 main characters and their personalities, and 4-5 "main themes." All of these sections are divided into headings based off the books and sections with a bullet point format. 

CoPilot: The very last section contains "Recurring themes", which shows the similarities between each of the six books in a social and moral context. There is also a small section about two of Austen's least popular novels: "Lady Susan" and "Sanditon."

Me: "What is the specific language that is being used throughout these works? Do they change styles each time? Or does it stay consistent with the time period that they were written in?"

CoPilot: "The short answer: Austen's language is remarkably consistent across her major novels, but each book subtly shifts style depending on its heroine, its thematic focus, and the kind of social world it's portraying. She writes conventions of the early 19th-century Regency prose, but she bends those conventions in distinct ways."

CoPilot: Goes into detail on what Regency-era standard English is by including some keywords: "imprudence," "felicity," "disposition", "principle," "conduct," "propriety," "sensibility," "I should be exceedingly obliged."

CoPilot: Lists more of her personal writing style by listing senses of irony, satire, morality, and clarity. Then, each book is broken down based on its publishing date chronologically and shows how the language is different in each one. To sum up each analysis of the book, there is a little section called "Tone", where key words are listed to confirm said analysis (e.g., Mansfield Park: Tone: "'austere, ethical, psychologically subtle.'")

CoPilot: Explains why Austen changes her language: "[Tailoring the] narration to the heroine's consciousness... adapts to genre conventions... [and] she [evolved] as a writer."

CoPilot: Sums up all points with the typical, short and sweet "Final Takeaway."

**My Personal Analysis**

Voyant's results told me a basic outline of the type of language Austen uses in a visual way, which I appreciate because I like the aesthetic side of things. CoPilot was more detailed, structured as a "close read" with its list of characters, themes, relationships, etc. I already knew what close reading was, so I wasn't surprised when CoPilot pulled out a ton of information even though I didn't put a link to any of the books to analyze, but now I have first-hand experience on how distant reading works. Voyant is great when you have a big chunk of text to analyze in a short amount of time-- CoPilot and any platform similar limits you based on how big your file is-- and I really appreciate the visual graphs, charts, and that Word Cloud. However, I would not recommend this platform to someone who is trying to get that deeper analysis or needs more context to understand a work.
